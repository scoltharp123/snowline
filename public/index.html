How Snowline works

Snowline blends up to nine weather models (ECMWF IFS and AIFS, GFS, HRRR, NBM, GEM, ICON, UKMO, JMA) with the ECMWF and GFS ensembles. It doesn't use each model's built-in snowfall. It re-models snow at your elevation instead: it adjusts temperature for altitude, finds the rain/snow line from the freezing level, and applies a temperature-based snow-to-liquid ratio. ECMWF, HRRR and NBM get extra weight in the blend.

Days run 6am–6am (Day 6a–6p, Night 6p–6a). Ski surfaces are modeled hour by hour from new snow, density, wind, sun, melt-freeze and rain, and each hour gets its own score for alpine, skate and classic. The wax calls are a starting point, so test on the snow.

Backcountry and avalanches: Snowline is a weather tool. It does not assess avalanche danger, does not make recommendations, and never says a slope, route or day is safe. Forecasts and map layers (slope angle, aspect, wind loading) are estimates and can be wrong. Avalanche forecasts shown are the avalanche center's own, unchanged. Anyone entering avalanche terrain is solely responsible for their decisions and should read the avalanche center's forecast, carry rescue gear and have avalanche training. Snowline and its maker accept no responsibility for how this information is used.

Data: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), ERA5/Copernicus, NOAA, avalanche.org, RainViewer, OpenStreetMap/OpenTopoMap. These are model estimates, not avalanche or safety guidance.
