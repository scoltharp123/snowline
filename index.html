How Snowline works. We pull up to nine deterministic weather models (ECMWF IFS, ECMWF AIFS, GFS, HRRR, NBM, GEM, ICON, UKMO, JMA) plus the ECMWF (51 members) and GFS (31 members) ensembles from Open-Meteo. We don't use each model's built-in snowfall. Instead, Snowline re-models snow at whatever elevation you choose: it adjusts temperature by the standard lapse rate, finds the rain/snow line from the model freezing level (snow level ≈ 1,000 ft below it), and applies a temperature-based snow-to-liquid ratio (Kuchera-style, 4:1 to 22:1). That way the base and summit get different, realistic totals. The consensus is a weighted blend (ECMWF, HRRR and NBM weighted higher). Days run 6am–6am, split into Day (6a–6p) and Night (6p–6a), so a morning powder day shows up on the day you'd ski it.
Ski surfaces are modeled hour by hour from new snow, how fluffy or dense it is, settling, wind loading, sun and heat, melt-freeze cycles (firm → corn) and rain crusts. Each hour gets separate 0–10 scores for alpine, skate and classic. Wax calls follow standard temperature and snow-type bands; they're a starting point, so test on the snow. Season history uses ERA5 (Copernicus) and Open-Meteo historical forecasts. The extended outlook uses the NOAA GFS 35-day ensemble and Open-Meteo's seasonal models. Avalanche danger comes from avalanche.org and radar from RainViewer. Ridgetop wind is the model's free-air wind (850–700 hPa) interpolated to your elevation.
